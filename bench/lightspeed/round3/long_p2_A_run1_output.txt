### The Core Lifecycle: Ingestion, Transformation, and Storage

Ingestion is described not merely as a passive receipt of data, but as an active process involving **nodes** and **stages**. This distributed nature implies that ingestion is parallelized, requiring coordination to ensure that data arriving from various sources is captured accurately.

Once ingested, data undergoes **transformation**. This is the most frequently mentioned operational activity in the log, appearing in contexts such as "transformation processes," "transformation stages," and "transformation nodes." Transformation is the engine of the pipeline, converting raw, unstructured, or semi-structured data into a usable format. The log indicates that transformation is not a single step but a series of stages, often occurring across different nodes. This distribution allows for scalability but introduces complexity, as each transformation stage must handle data consistently.

Following transformation, data moves to **storage**. The log references "storage processes," "storage nodes," and "storage stages," indicating that storage is not a monolithic repository but a distributed system itself. The interaction between transformation and storage is critical; data must be written efficiently to storage nodes while maintaining integrity. The frequent pairing of "storage" with "throughput" and "latency" highlights that storage performance is a bottleneck that directly impacts the overall speed of the pipeline.
